Juin 1944 — Sheet 2

Trungy, 27 juin. — Contacted 50th Div and 7th Armoured I.O. Recce for a place to hold a cinema for front line troops.

Trungy, 28 juin. — Located Mayor of Tille-sur-Seulles at Bayeux. Visited Tilly, located ditch with 18 dead civilians therein. Another woman dead on street. Town uninhabited and badly demolished. Arranged for a group of former residents of Tilly now living in Bayeux to identify and bury the civilians. Question of dead cattle arose and it was deemed a purely civilian problem. Arranged for civilian policing of Tilly. Met with mayor of Buceels on local problems and arranged for local citizens to collect and drive cattle, sheep, dogs and chickens what had strayed away from Bernieres-Bocage. The medical offr. visited and examined 50 refugees quartered in Trungy, found them to be in good condition, clean and well fed. Contacted 7th Armoured and 50th Divs. relative to movements. Many questions of pillage mentioned by civilians.

Trungy, 29 juin. — Transported civilians from Bayeux to Tilly-sur-Seulles to identify and bury deceased civilians. Picked up 20 (twenty) wireless sets in Tilly which had been abandoned.

Juin 1944 — Sheet 3

CA Adm Camp HQ / Reviers / Secqueville-en-Bessin, 26 juin, 1800. — Proceeded under orders to Advance HQ 15th Scottish Div at Cully but were sent from there to Rear HQ 1/2 miles South of St. Gabriel arriving there 1800. Pitched camp there. Ordered to St. Mauvieux to establish our detachment HQ. Left at 1723. Engine trouble made necessary that our 15 cwt stop at REME, Secqueville-en-Bessin, for repairs. Capt. Potter, Driver Gunnel and Batman Buckley remained there pending repairs. Also Lt. Mullens with motorcycle. Rest of detachment and 3 tonner proceeded to destination. Major deGraves and the rest of the detachment arrived in St. Mauvieux about 1800 hours. Village was still under mortar and shell fire and snipers were reported in further end of village which was badly damaged. The mayor and other officials had gone and only three families totalling 14 people remained, one of whom was wounded. They had been without food for two days. Two bodies, one male and one female were in the ruins of houses, Civilians remaining were: Louis Rousin, wife and two children; Eugene Larose, wife and two children; M. Lepaule and two children; Mme. Lepaule (grandmother) and Emile Larose and wife. As a counter-attack was developing and no habitable buildings remained all civilians were evacuated to Secqueville-en-Bessin for the night and given permits for travel to families in the area where they will be accommodated. The wounded man was sent by ambulance to Bayeux. St. Mauvieux was completely evacuated and there is no accommodation available there. No refugees remain in the area and there is no refugee movement. Traffic by road is satisfactory. Rations were left with refugees. Major de Graves and detachment came back with the 3 ton lorry to Secqueville-en-Bessin and the entire detachment camped for the night in an open sided hay barn at this place. REME worked until nearly midnight on the 15 cwt and finished the job next morning. They reported that the lorry should never have been passed for overseas service.

Bretteville-Norrey RR station, 27 juin, 1500. — Left under orders from Secqueville-en-Bessin and arrived at Bretteville-Norrey Railroad station at 1500 hours. While we were being established we were handed orders from 8th Corps to be relieved by Detachment 218, originally assigned to this district but held up on arrival. Detachment 218 arrived and our detachment left at 1610 for 8th Corps Rear HQ. Standing by at this destination.

Lantheuil, 28 juin. — Remained in camp. No further instructions. Weather unsettled as it had been last two days. Roads muddy. But better today.

Lantheuil, 29 juin. — Heavy firing near here by our guns. Heavy rains.

Lantheuil, 30 juin. — Our 15cwt lorry sent with Detachment 217 to Division HQ to assist them in getting their transportation and G-1098 stores which they were compelled to abandon because of German counter-attack at their original assignment. Upon arrival the 3 ton lorry had been located and 15cwt of our detachment returned to camp.

Trungy, 1er juillet. — Posted proclamations at Livre, Ste. Honorine de Ducy & Ellon. Removed historical effects, 5 truck loads, from Chateua near Lingevres to Bayeux at request of Div. Routine passes to civilians.

Trungy, 2 juillet. — 50th Div. notified us of necessity for removal of civilians from south of Tilly-la Belle Epine road. Arranged for receiving of refugees in area North.

Trungy, 3 juillet. — Contacted Prefect de Police Bayeux relative to missing person Tilly area. Met with 7 Armoured Div. relative to payment of civilian claims on damage. Visited by Major Brunow (213) 2 Armoured Div. (U.S.) who join us on the right. Met with 50 Div., 22 Armoured Bde and 1st Tank Corps at their request relative to payment of claims to civilians. Major Macrae called on the Mayors of Juaye-Mondaye, Jerusalem, Folliot, D'Ellon, Cahagnolles, Quesnay-Guesnon, Parfouru-l'Eclin to obtain information about conditions, and to supply them with price control lists. Two civilians namely Adrien Marie and Jules Delauney were left with us overnight for safekeeping by F.S.S. 50 Div. They had been passing below our lines and were suspected of subversive activities. A female, Laulotte Bouet had been noticed by our detachment officers in the community in several wide spread parts of the area and also was suspected by local citizens of suspicious activities. She was held for questioning by FSS 50 Corps. They checked and released her, French Authorities are noting her activities.

Octobre 1944 — Sheet 1

Elst, 1er octobre. — Railway trucks in ELST Station set on fire by continuous shelling. Major Wright and Capt Parker endeavoured to get assistance from 43 Divn, and Army Fire Service at NIJMEGEN to deal with same. No help available. Major Wright & Capt Parker tried to blow up one truck and so isolate fire. Charge did not go off. These officers then got assistance of volunteers from Stn. Staff in charge of Stationmaster to manhandle trucks, and while 15 trucks were burnt out the remainder amounting to about 150 were saved. The town and Station was being heavily shelled all the time. Message received from the Burgomaster that it was believed enemy were advancing towards ELST, and the police wished to evacuate town. Major Macrae & Major Reitz went to Town Hall, calmed the Officials and persuaded the Police to remain at their posts. Remained there until the shelling had ceased.

Elst, 2 octobre. — Burgomaster of ELST moved his office to Det. H.Q. as Town Hall was damaged by shell fire.

MULLENS at AUNEY SUR ODON, Le BENY BOCAGE, Le MESNIL AUZOUF, VASSY & CONDE SUR NOIREAU. Gendarmerie deficiencies of Transport, Stationery, clothing and buildings reported to 5 Sub Area L OF C.

Carville, 2 septembre. — Major de GRAVES and Major KRATZ for FALAISE and Adm. H.Q. Returned with Mail - the first for many days. A number of pairs of boots were obtained from R.S.D. and distributed to Gendarmes who were in great need of clothing and footwear.

Carville, 3 septembre. — Canton Mayors of AUNEY SUR ODON, VASSY, LE BENY BOCAGE, CONDE SUR NOIREAU contacted by Capt. KERIN & Lt. MULLENS who were advised that all available civilians were employed, that the only problems remaining were re-housing and shortages of food, allocated supplies from French Authorities being considerably delayed. Capt. SHEPHERD, Sup. Offr. ordered to report to 2300 Army. Received "Stand by" orders for immediate movement and to turn over responsibility for area to French Authorities.

Carville, 4 septembre. — Quantities of urgently required food delivered to Mayors of St. JEAN LE BLANC, LASSY, DANVOIR, VASSY, CONDE SUR NOIREAU and MONTCHAMPS. Visit from Col. USHER and Col. HYLES of CAEN Detachment.

Carville, 5 septembre. — Capt. SHEPHERD, Sup. Offr. left to rejoin Army. Quantities of biscuits delivered to Mayors of Le BENY BOCAGE, ST. MARTIN DES BESSAGES and CARVILLE. All biscuit stocks now exhausted with the exception of 12 tins placed in Det. reserve. French civilians at LE BENY BOCAGE and VASSY have heard of our impending departure and have expressed deep concern over future of France, fearing clashes between F.F.I. and Authorities. General Public have little confidence in F.F.I., alleging that most members are of Communist tendencies. Received inf. from 5 L of C. that we are to leave this area in next 7 days. Detailed instr. to follow.

Septembre 1944 — War Diary (Sheet 3 : 12 - 14 Septembre)

En Route, 12 septembre (1330). — Left LILLE via YPRES - MENIN to 4th Cdn Div. just outside BRUGES arr. Div. Main at 1250 hrs. Advised to contact Rear. Maj. de GRAVES contacted office of A.A. & Q.M.G. where he was instructed to take over Civil Affairs matters in BRUGES. Entered BRUGES 1330 hrs. Maj. de GRAVES immediately contacted Burgomeister, M. VAN HOESTENBERGE, Victorian, and made prelim. arrangements for facilities and contacts. Major de GRAVES also made contact with Major STUBBS, 221 Det., who had made prelim recce of BRUGES and who handed to us his prelim report.

Bruges, 13 septembre. — More detailed recce of BRUGES showed that morale of Public is particularly high. Water good and plentiful. Communications - poor as 21 of Town's Bridges had been blown by retreating Germans, and neutralising most road and rail traffic. There is no electricity or gas, but arrangements have already been made today for the Electric Grid system from BRUSSELS to be extended to BRUGES to replace destroyed facilities normally obtained from OSTEND. Food situation - fairly good, but all economic home life is based on a Black Market. However, price of Butter has today dropped from 500 fcs. per kilo to 100, but at this latter price is almost impossible to obtain. Received visit from Brigadier MONSALL and Lt. Col. PROCTOR. Contact effected with Municipal Authorities, Belgian Military (Major Von RONSTADT), Provost (Lt. TWEDDLE) and Field Security (Capt. ASHBY).

Bruges, 14 septembre. — Hospital recce carried out. Lengthy interviews today with Commissioner Chef of Police Municipal and Captain of Gendarmerie for W. Flanders. Military requirements stressed, particularly that with Provost and Q. (Movements). Full co-operation promised. All Police and Gendarmes are well equipped with the exception that they have few weapons and there is a deficiency of transport requirements. Contact made with Prison Governor. Prison inspected. No Political Prisoners. Arrangements with Police and Gendarmes for posting of Notices drawing the attention of children to unusual articles lying in streets and fields with a view to prevention of accidents in handling of abandoned war equipment.

Information received respecting Glider crashing on outskirts of Town and instruments and equipment intact although crew had gone to BRUSSELS. Glider guarded and F/Lt. EMERSON (RAF Int) advised.

Bruges, 26 septembre. — Labour survey by Capt. DWYER. Review of security restrictions in company with Capt. ASHBY, F.S., and it was decided to keep our rigid control on traffic in and out of the city.

Bruges, 27 septembre. — Loophole of Security regulations noticed at Rly. Stn. Arrangements tightened, resulting in more applications for travel exempts. permits.

Septembre 1944 — War Diary (Sheet 6 : 28 - 30 Septembre)

Bruges, 28 septembre. — Town appears to have at least resumed normal life and work.

Bruges, 29 septembre. — Lt. MULLANT and Mr. CORLEUR both Belgian Citizens have lost their lives today while clearing Mines. The accident was reported by our 3 Ton Lorry Driver who had worked with them for the past 10 days. A letter of condolence from Major De GRAVES was handed to the Military Governor by Major PAULEZ.

Bruges, 30 septembre. — Our 3 Ton Truck has been engaged on almost every day in assisting Belgian Military authorities in collection of dumps of German Military equipment and munitions left and abandoned here. Received visit from Col. FRASER, W.A. 12th Cdn. Gen Hosp. with matters regarding billeting of 90 Nurses and civilian employment. Col. FRASER was referred to Town Major with regard to Billeting.

Signé par : W.A. de Graves, Major (O.C. 223 A Det C.A.)
